## The responsibility

At Profitable Ideas Exchange, I established the firm's AI capabilities and continue to direct its strategy, investments and adoption. My formal role is Director. The work includes technology evaluation, vendor decisions, governance, pilots, employee training and workflow redesign.

I lead an AI Task Force involving the CFO and Chief Client Officer. I present recommendations, progress and investment proposals to senior leadership and the Board. That puts implementation decisions in the same conversation as operating priorities and investment.

## Turning strategy into working practices

The work extends beyond choosing tools. I build AI agents and automations, run pilots and work with teams on how the technology fits into their daily responsibilities. Technology decisions, quality expectations and organizational adoption need to be considered together.

With client delivery leadership, I helped establish a Center of Excellence focused on more consistent client deliverables. I developed AI agents with embedded quality controls. Directors who use those agents report improvements in quality, productivity, consistency and capacity. Use varies across directors.

## The judgment behind the work

I evaluate AI in the context of a specific workflow. A useful decision needs to account for the work being supported, the quality expected, the review required and the effort of adopting a new process.

My approach is to define the baseline and success criteria before expanding a pilot. A promising demonstration is a reason to test a workflow, rather than evidence that an organization should adopt it broadly. Human review, failure handling and ownership belong in the design of the system.

These are the principles I use to assess deployment. They are not a claim that every pilot produced the same result, or that a particular vendor decision caused the capacity figure below.

## Capacity released

Workflow, automation and training initiatives released the equivalent of **4.6 project coordinator full-time positions in capacity**, calculated using hours saved over a **48-week working year**.

The calculation expresses annual hours released as a proportion of the annual working hours of one project coordinator:

> FTE-equivalent capacity = annual hours released ÷ annual working hours per coordinator

The annualization uses 48 working weeks. This is a reported internal capacity estimate. The underlying time records and weekly full-time hours assumption are not reproduced on this site, so the figure is not presented as an independently audited calculation.

The result describes time available for other work. It does not represent a reduction in headcount or verified cash savings.

## Adoption and evidence

Approximately **75 employees use AI tools**, including Microsoft Copilot. That figure covers AI tool use; it does not mean every employee uses the custom agents. Feedback from directors using the agents is distinct from a measured result across every team.

The work described here is at Profitable Ideas Exchange. Internal workflows, client deliverables and executive discussions are not reproduced. The independent technical projects elsewhere on this site are separate from this employer work.
